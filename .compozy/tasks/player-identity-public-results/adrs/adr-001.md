# ADR-001: Keep the Player Workspace Optional and Additive

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-08-03

## Context

MTGO Opponent Notes already has opponent profiles, encounter-driven capture, and
opponent-bound public-deck enrichment. The new workflow represents the person
using the application and their source-attributed public results. Requiring this
identity during onboarding would make an optional enrichment capability a gate
for the existing local notebook and would blur Player and opponent ownership.

## Decision

Add a discoverable top-level Player workspace whose setup is completely optional.
The notebook, capture, overlay, opponent history, and opponent enrichment remain
usable without a Player identity. The Player workflow owns at most one local
identity per notebook and never creates, aliases, or mutates opponent records.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Require Player identity during application onboarding

- **Description**: Block normal application use until a nickname is saved.
- **Pros**: Ensures every installation has Player data before using enrichment.
- **Cons**: Adds friction, makes an external-data feature feel mandatory, and
  couples unrelated local notebook behavior to Player setup.
- **Why rejected**: The approved capability is additive and must not obstruct the
  existing privacy-first workflow.

### Alternative 2: Require setup when the Player tab opens

- **Description**: Turn the Player tab into a blocking setup screen.
- **Pros**: Keeps global onboarding unchanged while forcing a defined identity.
- **Cons**: Prevents users from inspecting source disclosures and understanding
  the workflow before deciding whether to participate.
- **Why rejected**: First-use guidance and provider status should remain visible
  without coercing identity creation.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Existing workflows remain unchanged and regression boundaries stay explicit.
- Users may evaluate disclosures and source availability before saving a nickname.
- Player-owned and opponent-owned records retain distinct meaning and lifecycle.

### Negative

- The Player workspace needs a useful first-use state with no saved identity.
- Commands must handle an absent Player identity as a normal product state.

### Risks

- Navigation or copy could still imply setup is required; acceptance coverage must
  verify that every existing workflow remains usable with no Player identity.

## Implementation Notes

The TechSpec must define a distinct Player aggregate and capability surface. It
may reuse generic infrastructure but must not reuse opponent profiles, encounter
triggers, opponent snapshots, or opponent command authority.
