# ADR-002: Use Explicit Conditional and Manual Public Source Routes

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-08-03

## Context

Official MTGO surfaces publish limited decklist and seasonal leaderboard data,
but no supported general player-profile, complete match-history, or Decklists API
contract was found. Daybreak Census exposes a narrow MTGO leaderboard collection
and requires a Service ID whose distributed-desktop use, quota, and current policy
must be reviewed before production access. Unsupported automation would weaken the
product's trust and may violate source terms.

## Decision

Use Daybreak Census MOCS leaderboard data as the sole conditional machine-readable
V1 provider. Keep it disabled and make zero requests unless all production gates
pass. Provide official MTGO as an explicit system-browser handoff and a
user-attested manual evidence source. Provide MTGTop8 only as an optional,
separately consented browser corroboration handoff. Never scrape, parse browser
pages, or contact/open a fallback provider automatically.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Automate official Decklists discovery

- **Description**: Fetch or parse the public Decklists site behind its player search.
- **Pros**: Richer discovery with less manual entry.
- **Cons**: No reviewed API or automation contract; HTML and absence semantics are
  unstable; automation would create policy and privacy risk.
- **Why rejected**: V1 requires documented permission and a stable contract before
  machine access.

### Alternative 2: Aggregate third-party result sites

- **Description**: Ingest results from popular deck and tournament sites.
- **Pros**: Broader coverage and redundancy.
- **Cons**: Authorization, attribution, identity matching, and completeness vary;
  automatic fallback would disclose data without a source-specific choice.
- **Why rejected**: The researched sources do not meet the V1 permission and
  reliability boundary.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Every external disclosure is explicit, destination-specific, and understandable.
- The complete local/manual workflow remains available when Census is disabled.
- Production cannot silently drift onto an unsupported endpoint or source.

### Negative

- Discovery is narrower and may require browser or manual work.
- Census support can be implemented locally yet remain unavailable in production.

### Risks

- Source policy or schema may change; expiry-bound configuration and live evidence
  gates keep stale assumptions fail-closed.
- Users may interpret scoped absence as complete history; UI copy must preserve the
  provider-and-time-limited meaning.

## Implementation Notes

The Census adapter must be testable against synthetic fixtures while disabled.
Provider enablement requires a reviewed Service ID/use model, current policy and
quota evidence, expiry-bound scope configuration, attribution, and live contract
verification. Official and third-party browser routes must be host-built and
separately consented.
