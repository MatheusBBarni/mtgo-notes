# ADR-003: Preserve Immutable Player-Owned Public Result Evidence

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-08-03

## Context

A public result is a source-scoped statement about an exact nickname, not proof of
account ownership, a natural person, name continuity, or complete history. MTGO
nicknames can change or be recycled. Existing public-deck snapshots belong to
opponent encounters and cannot represent the application owner's evidence without
mixing ownership and provenance.

## Decision

Store imported public results as a distinct Player-owned, immutable, typed evidence
model. Exact case-insensitive nickname matching is the only V1 match. Every preview
and import preserves source identity, attribution, lookup/source nicknames, scope,
observation time, payload kind, source digest, and preview digest. Duplicate source
statements create nothing new; changed statements become linked immutable versions.
The player may change only which optional fields are retained, recorded as an
append-only selection revision. Nickname edits never rekey, alias, or relink history.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Reuse opponent deck records and public snapshots

- **Description**: Store Player results in the existing encounter-bound schema.
- **Pros**: Fewer new concepts and potential reuse of existing views.
- **Cons**: Falsifies ownership, requires encounter relationships, and risks Player
  actions mutating opponent history.
- **Why rejected**: Player evidence and opponent enrichment are separate bounded
  workflows with different triggers, provenance, and lifecycle.

### Alternative 2: Store editable result notes

- **Description**: Let users freely edit imported source content after saving.
- **Pros**: Easy correction and flexible annotation.
- **Cons**: Destroys the distinction between sourced facts and personal notes and
  makes deduplication/change detection unauditable.
- **Why rejected**: Corrections must produce new evidence versions; personal notes
  remain a separate editable feature.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Users can tell exactly what a source stated and when it was observed or attested.
- Public evidence remains auditable across nickname changes, refreshes, and consent
  revocation.
- Deduplication and source-change behavior are deterministic.

### Negative

- The product needs typed schemas, version links, selection history, and dedicated
  deletion/portability behavior.
- Manual corrections require a new preview/version rather than in-place editing.

### Risks

- A generic “verified” label could overstate identity certainty; UI and schemas must
  retain explicit provenance modes and scoped language.
- Partial deck data could appear authoritative; V1 rejects partial card lists and
  classifies only complete validated decklist evidence.

## Implementation Notes

The TechSpec must define independent identity, evidence, selection, empty-outcome,
and tombstone storage plus exact source-key/digest canonicalization. Player evidence
must never be inserted into opponent profile, encounter, deck, or snapshot records.
