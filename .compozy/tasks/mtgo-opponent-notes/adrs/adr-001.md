# ADR-001: Stage the Opponent-Memory V1 Around a Tournament-Conservative Core

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-07-28

## Context

The product will help an individual Magic: The Gathering Online player capture subjective observations during a match and recall them across later encounters. The confirmed concept includes private local data, free-form notes, deck identity, observed cards, play tendencies, a dated "last deck seen" summary, and a fast companion capture surface.

Current MTGO tools already provide automated match tracking, opponent history, and overlays. The differentiated value is therefore trustworthy first-person memory rather than another deck tracker. The MTGO EULA also gives Daybreak broad discretion over third-party programs that affect gameplay or collect information from the game. Manual entry without process, memory, file, or log access is a lower-risk boundary, but the product cannot claim official tournament safety without written approval.

The user requested both a tournament-conservative behavior and a casual behavior that exposes the full historical dossier during play, as well as an Extra Turn-like overlay. The council found that launching two disclosure modes and committing to an overlay would combine the recall, interaction, and policy hypotheses before the core habit is validated.

## Decision

V1 will validate one private, local-first opponent-memory loop:

- Store dated encounters and user-authored observations as historical records.
- Support free-form notes plus lightweight deck identity, observed-card, and play-tendency fields.
- Derive a clearly dated "last deck seen" summary without presenting it as the opponent's current deck.
- Launch with one fail-closed behavior labeled **Tournament-conservative**, not "tournament-safe." Historical notes are available before a match, between games, and after a match; during a game, only observations created in that current match are exposed.
- Require the primary capture flow to achieve a median completion time below five seconds. Compare a focused mini-window with a compact overlay during prototyping and ship the simplest surface that reliably meets the target.
- Support narrow, encrypted manual backup and restore while deferring readable export and sharing.

Casual full-dossier access remains a planned follow-up after the core recall habit and disclosure boundary are validated.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Launch the Overlay with Tournament-Conservative and Casual Modes

- **Description**: Ship the requested compact overlay and let users switch between restricted historical visibility and full in-match dossier access.
- **Pros**: Delivers the complete requested experience; offers immediate flexibility; tests demand for both behaviors.
- **Cons**: Combines three separate bets; increases policy, mode-confusion, UX, and test surface; makes weak adoption harder to diagnose.
- **Why rejected**: The council prioritized validating repeat-opponent recall and safe disclosure before adding a second mode.

### Alternative 2: Ship a Generic Free-Form Opponent Notebook

- **Description**: Store one mutable note per opponent without dated encounters, structured observations, or disclosure distinctions.
- **Pros**: Fastest initial delivery; minimal product surface.
- **Cons**: Weak differentiation; poor provenance; stale claims can appear current; future history and visibility features require disruptive migration.
- **Why rejected**: It does not create trustworthy longitudinal memory or support the required current-versus-historical boundary.

### Alternative 3: Build an Automated MTGO Tracker

- **Description**: Read MTGO logs or other client data to identify opponents, decks, cards, and match state automatically.
- **Pros**: Reduces manual entry; resembles established tracker and overlay expectations.
- **Cons**: Competes directly with existing tools; materially increases policy and enforcement risk; dilutes the subjective-memory differentiator.
- **Why rejected**: Automation is unnecessary to test the V1 value hypothesis and exceeds the lower-risk product boundary.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Isolates the core hypothesis: players repeatedly capture and recall subjective opponent knowledge.
- Establishes provenance and historical truth from the first release.
- Keeps disclosure rules consistent across future capture surfaces.
- Reduces MTGO integration and privacy risk.
- Preserves accumulated user value through constrained backup and restore.

### Negative

- Does not initially deliver the requested casual full-dossier experience.
- May delay a polished overlay if a simpler mini-window meets the capture target.
- Requires explicit match-phase transitions unless a later approved mechanism can identify them safely.
- Manual opponent identification and note entry add friction.

### Risks

- **Unsupported compliance inference**: Users may treat "Tournament-conservative" as approval. Mitigate with clear disclaimers and no claim of official safety.
- **Mode leakage**: Historical content may appear during a game. Mitigate by enforcing disclosure policy outside individual UI components and failing closed when match state is uncertain.
- **Stale observations**: Historical deck or behavior data may be mistaken for current truth. Mitigate with timestamps, provenance labels, and correction controls.
- **Identity fragmentation**: Handle changes or entry mistakes may split an opponent's history. Mitigate with merge and correction workflows.
- **Data exposure**: Backups may leave protected local storage. Mitigate with authenticated encryption, explicit user action, and no plaintext export.

## Implementation Notes

The later TechSpec should compare Tauri and Electrobun for Windows overlay reliability, focus behavior, packaging, accessibility, and team familiarity. The idea phase does not select the framework.

The domain should keep encounter records, historical observations, current-match observations, and derived summaries distinct so that every UI surface uses the same disclosure rules.
